Transitivity Property

Use a chain of asymptotic relationships to conclude how the first function compares with the third.

1. What is transitivity?

Transitivity lets us connect two comparisons. If A relates to B, and B relates to C in the required way, we can conclude a relationship between A and C.

f(n) = O(g(n))
g(n) = O(h(n))
Therefore: f(n) = O(h(n))

This rule also works for Θ and Ω:

  • If f = Θ(g) and g = Θ(h), then f = Θ(h).
  • If f = Ω(g) and g = Ω(h), then f = Ω(h).
Easy analogy: If A is no taller than B, and B is no taller than C, then A is no taller than C.

3. Worked example

Let f(n)=n, g(n)=n², and h(n)=n³.

  1. n = O(n²) because n grows no faster than n².
  2. n² = O(n³) because n² grows no faster than n³.
  3. Therefore, by transitivity, n = O(n³).
Important: Keep the same type of relationship in the chain. For example, two Big-O relationships lead to a Big-O conclusion. For mixed relationships, check the exact rule rather than assuming every combination works.
